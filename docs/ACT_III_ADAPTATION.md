# Act III compact episode: source and adaptation notes

## Witness and scope

This episode uses the Andhra Bharati transcription of the **1909 second-edition first printing** of *Kanyasulkam*. The [Act III scene 1 text](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/31.html), [scene 2](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/32.html), [scene 3](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/33.html), and [scene 4](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/34.html) supply the episode's sequence and plot facts. This is a different witness from the **1961-based text used for the Act I Telugu UI pilot**; do not transfer its wording, scene divisions, or page references to Act III or treat the witnesses as interchangeable.

This is one compact playable episode, not a complete Act III or full-play adaptation. It moves through four source locations in order: Ramappantulu's front room, his bedroom, outside Agnihotravadhani's house, and the garden. The locations are compressed into one continuous side-scroll route.

## Text facts used

The following are plot facts from the selected 1909 witness, not new game mechanics:

- **Scene 1, Ramappantulu's front room:** Madhuravani challenges Ramappantulu's claims about his means and their relationship, naming the mortgaged land and his evasions. She refuses money and unwanted intimacy and demands that he stop arranging the older Lubdhavadhani's marriage. The text has Ramappantulu force a kiss after her refusal; that is his violation, not her consent.
- **Scene 2, Ramappantulu's bedroom:** Karataka Śāstri appears in disguise with his male disciple disguised as a girl and asks Madhuravani for help with the proposed marriage involving Subbi. Madhuravani joins the scheme and steers it through Ramappantulu's self-serving legal methods and the astrologer. The disguise carries risk for the disciple.
- **Scene 3, outside Agnihotravadhani's house:** Gireesam privately weighs widow marriage and reform against his own advantage, then approaches Buchchamma under cover of a lesson.
- **Scene 4, the garden:** Gireesam improvises a pseudo-educational lesson about widow marriage and social reform while courting Buchchamma. His public principles and private calculations do not match; the scene also includes Venkatesam and a comic dispute over fruit.

## Adaptation choices

The route uses a silent observer marker rather than making the player speak for Gireesam, Madhuravani, or Buchchamma. Madhuravani anchors the episode. The only multi-option interaction asks the player to compare Ramappantulu's respectable claims with his conduct and notice who bears the consequences. Both readings lead into the same plot: neither cancels Madhuravani's refusal, changes her decision to help, or grants the player authority over another person's consent.

The front-room interaction names the unwanted touch without staging it as romance or a playable action. At the bedroom stop, the player cannot expose, dispatch, or turn the disguised disciple into a tool. The outside-house and garden stops show Gireesam's self-interest and improvised teaching; humor belongs to his sham respectability and unreliable lesson, not to Buchchamma's questions, any woman's refusal, or coercion. His courtship earns no reward, and this route does not invent Buchchamma's answer.

All game text is original English adaptation, not translation or quotation. No Telugu dialogue, transliteration, or machine-translated line is added. The single branching interaction is a new interpretive mechanic, not a choice found in the play. The standard three-stage escape campaign and the established Act I and Act II story routes remain separate.

## Contributor safeguards

- Keep the 1909 second-edition first-printing witness separate from the 1961-based witness used for Act I Telugu UI; verify the chosen witness before adding any quotation.
- Do not add Telugu dialogue or transliteration without exact witness verification and fluent review; do not quote OCR alone.
- Keep Madhuravani's boundaries and plan hers. Never make money, intimacy, or coercion a player reward or joke.
- Keep Subbi a person whose future is at stake, not a prize, collectible, route gate, or mission object. Do not let a choice rewrite her wishes or the play's events.
- Keep the disguised disciple's risk visible; do not reward exposure, coercion, or using him as a disposable tool.
- Keep the episode compact and label later claims or mechanics as adaptation rather than source fact.

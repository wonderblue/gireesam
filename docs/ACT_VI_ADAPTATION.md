# Act VI compact episode: source and adaptation notes

## Witness and scope

This episode follows Andhra Bharati's transcription of the **1909 second-edition first printing** of *Kanyasulkam*. The consulted pages are [scene 61](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/61.html), [62](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/62.html), [63](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/63.html), [64](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/64.html), [65](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/65.html), [66](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/66.html), and [67](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/67.html). This witness is distinct from the **1961-based witness used for the Act I Telugu UI**; scene divisions and page references must not be interchanged.

This is one compact, five-stop playable episode, not a full Act VI or a full-play adaptation. It compresses scenes 61–67 into a silent observer's evidence-and-resource chain, with Madhuravani and Sowjanya's choices guiding the ethical perspective. The player's selections organize a record; they do not decide guilt, safety, ownership, or a case outcome.

## Source facts used

- **Scenes 61–62:** Agnihotravadhani and Ramappantulu discover a mismatch in the wedding arrangements. The dispute escalates into a claim that Gireesam carried off a girl and property. This episode treats the missing-disciple accusation as an **allegation only**: the party's confusion, search orders, and circulated account do not establish an abduction or the identity of the person sought.
- **Scene 63:** Litigation talk brings borrowed money, a pawned ancestral bracelet, legal fees, attempts to influence a witness, and prepared defense language. Money and property reveal stakes and pressure; they do not prove the accusation. A coached, bought, or false account is never presented as firsthand observation.
- **Scene 64:** Lubdhavadhani regrets selling his own daughter Meenakshi in marriage to an old man. This is a separate family matter from the missing-disciple complaint. Meenakshi is not identified with the disciple and is not used as evidence, property, an obstacle, or a rescue token.
- **Scenes 65–66:** Karataka and his disciple face the consequences of the dispute. Madhuravani helps protect the disciple and presses toward an honest resolution rather than a contrived account. The game does not expose the disciple's private location or force testimony.
- **Scene 67:** Sowjanya refuses payment meant to influence proceedings and argues for reform, education, care, and the return of a daughter's own property. The episode keeps that household advice separate from both the missing-disciple allegation and Meenakshi's family matter.

## Adaptation choices

The original English game text is newly written, not a translation or quotation. No direct Telugu quotation, transliteration, or machine-translated dialogue is added. The five interaction cards distinguish direct conversation from hearsay; loans and pawns from proof; bribery and false testimony from observation; and separate family/property matters from the missing-disciple claim. The safe routes preserve privacy, avoid coached or forced testimony, and allow an honest account without promising an outcome.

The satire targets litigants who exploit money, status, and legal process, along with corrupt procedure—not the person being sought, Meenakshi, Madhuravani, or anyone with the least power. Meenakshi has no invented interior monologue or playable rescue role; her perspective is deliberately not supplied.

This story route is separate from the ordinary three-stage escape campaign and from Acts I–V. It ends before any subsequent resolution and does not add later-act events.

## Contributor safeguards

- Keep the missing-disciple report framed as an allegation, never an established abduction.
- Keep Karataka's disciple and Meenakshi as distinct people and distinct story threads. Never use Meenakshi as evidence, property in the case, an obstacle, or a rescue token.
- Preserve the chain between firsthand observation, hearsay, bribery, false or coached testimony, loans, pawned property, and the honest next step.
- Keep the daughter's property in Sowjanya's scene attached to the daughter it belongs to; property is not a substitute for consent or proof.
- Do not add Telugu dialogue or transliteration without exact wording verified against the named edition's page image and reviewed by a fluent Telugu speaker.
- Keep the episode a separate compact route; do not alter the ordinary campaign or disclose later-act outcomes.

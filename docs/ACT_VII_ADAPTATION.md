# Act VII compact finale: source and adaptation notes

## Witness and scope

This episode follows Andhra Bharati's transcription of the **1909 second-edition first printing** of *Kanyasulkam*, scenes [71](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/71.html), [72](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/72.html), [73](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/73.html), [74](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/74.html), [75](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/75.html), and [76](https://andhrabharati.com/nATakamulu/kanyASulkamu/76.html). This is distinct from the **1961-based witness used for the Act I Telugu UI**; the witnesses and their scene references are not interchangeable.

The game is one compact, five-stop, English-language finale. Its player is a silent observer who helps keep entries in the right evidentiary category. The player's selections do not decide guilt, expose a private location, turn a woman into an objective, or determine Buchchamma's future.

## Source facts used

- **Scenes 71–72:** The alleged missing girl and property are part of a confused and contested case. The filing and procedural exchange leave important particulars in dispute. Agnihotravadhani's bargain over Subbi and Lubdhavadhani's regret about selling his own daughter Meenakshi in marriage are separate family matters.
- **Scenes 73 and 75:** Witnesses describe pressure, coaching, and unreliable accounts. A witness's direct observation is different from a repeated report; testimony induced by payment or threats is not made reliable by entering it in a file. The play also presents an invented supernatural explanation that cannot establish what happened.
- **Scene 76:** The supposed missing girl or bride is Karataka Śāstri's male disciple in disguise, not Meenakshi or Buchchamma. Madhuravani has protected the disciple. Sowjanya Rao recognizes her help, rejects Gireesam's self-serving approach, and supports Buchchamma's education and freedom to decide her own future.

## Adaptation choices

The English dialogue is newly written, not a translation or quotation. No new direct Telugu line or machine translation is used. The playable evidence register separates direct observation, hearsay, bribed or coached testimony, and fabricated claims before the voluntary identification is recorded. The reveal is delivered as a person's identity, without a chase, gender gag, or spectacle. The source supplies the identity disclosure and protection; the game's "voluntary, uncoached" phrasing is consent-and-procedure framing added by this adaptation, not a claim that the play records sworn testimony.

The episode's procedural close—treating the missing-person/property charge as unsupported on the evidence in the scene—is an adaptation, not a claim that these pages record a formal court judgment. The identity question is resolved through a voluntary firsthand account; the disputed property claim stays separate and is not treated as established by that disclosure.

The two family matters remain distinct: Subbi is not part of the missing-person case, and Meenakshi is not identified with the disciple. Neither, nor Buchchamma or the disciple, becomes a mission object, prize, obstacle, or collectible. Gireesam earns no romantic or score reward. The ending belongs to Madhuravani, Sowjanya, and Buchchamma, and leaves Buchchamma's education and independent future in her hands.

This is a standalone Act VII story route. It does not alter the ordinary three-stage campaign or rewrite the preceding act episodes. Do not add Telugu dialogue or transliteration without verifying the exact wording against a named edition's page image and review by a fluent Telugu speaker.
